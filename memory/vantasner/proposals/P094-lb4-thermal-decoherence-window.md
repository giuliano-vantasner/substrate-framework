## Invariants, Conventions, and Allowed Imports

C-COH-001 proves that a supplied centered Gaussian phase of variance
`sigma^2` has mean phasor `exp(-sigma^2/2)` and pair coherence
`exp(-sigma^2)`. It explicitly derives no phase-noise dynamics. For a declared
Brownian phase increment with variance `2*D*t`, the corresponding mean-phasor
amplitude is `exp(-D*t)` and pair coherence is `exp(-2*D*t)`; these are
different observables and neither is automatically a survival probability or
population fraction.

C-SG-016 supplies deterministic normalized slow damping under conditional
family tracking. It gives exponential action but nonlinear finite-amplitude
energy and frequency, and no stochastic forcing, temperature, phase coordinate,
or physical lifetime. C-DYN-001 likewise separates abstract oscillator
transients, field modes, exact nonlinear existence, phase coherence, survival,
population, and discharge semantics. LB2's rejected `2*omega_b` reading cannot
be revived as a stochastic hard gate.

A fluctuation-dissipation theorem is not a coefficient slogan. It requires a
specified Langevin equation, damping convention, noise covariance, equilibrium
measure, stochastic-calculus convention, and units. Projecting one-coordinate
momentum noise onto oscillator phase produces a phase-dependent diffusion
coefficient before any fast-phase or weak-noise average. The factor and energy
dependence must be derived, not copied from the target visibility exponent.

Temperature, energy, action, frequency, and time remain normalized unless an
accepted physical map exists. Calling a scale `k_B*T` or `hbar*omega/2`
requires compatible units and a stated classical or quantum model; symbolic
endpoint limits do not establish that model. The source target 0.125 remains a
comparator and may not select a free temperature-to-energy ratio.
