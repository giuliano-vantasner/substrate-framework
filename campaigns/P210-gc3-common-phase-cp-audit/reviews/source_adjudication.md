# GC3 Source Adjudication

GC3 is qualified. Its common-phase matrix algebra is exact after every real
input and scalar-phase premise is stated, but its one-condensate, three-source,
geometry, and physical CP readings do not follow from accepted framework
authority.

## GC3.1 and GC3.2: Conditional Matrix Theorem

For any real finite matrix `R`, both Grams of `Y=exp(i theta)R` are real,
symmetric, positive semidefinite, and independent of theta. GC3 symbolically
checks the right Gram but then discusses the left singular basis. P210 proves
both rectangular identities and supplies the correctly typed real left basis.
Up and down matrices may carry different scalar global phases because each
phase cancels separately.

The algebra does not derive its physical premise. C-QBL-001 explicitly does
not force complex ontology, C-OVL-001 defines no Yukawa interaction or
condensate, and C-MIX-001 assigns no flavor or CKM identity. One scalar field
factor can multiply an independent complex coupling matrix, leaving a complex
Gram and a nonzero exact quartet. Position-dependent phase or complex mode
data also breaks the real-matrix premise.

## GC3.3: Null Quartets and Degeneracy

Real symmetric left Grams admit real orthogonal eigenbases, so their relative
basis can be chosen real and every quartet imaginary part vanishes. The 400
draws reproduce this exact identity but add no independent evidence. The
basis-aware commutator route is stronger: two real symmetric Grams have a real
antisymmetric commutator, every odd trace vanishes, and in odd dimension its
determinant vanishes.

Repeated spectra retain larger unitary freedoms. The identity Gram can be
diagonalized by the complex Fourier basis, whose coordinate quartet is
nonzero, while the commutator is still zero. P210 therefore retains the
existence of a real representative and the null invariants without treating
an arbitrary degenerate coordinate basis as physical CP.

## GC3.4 and GC3.5: Multi-Source Examples

The random and external-well assemblies are reproducible finite examples in
which sums of differently phased real matrices produce nonzero quartets. A
positive minimum among 400 draws is not a mathematical bound away from zero,
and aligned real source matrices are counterexamples to any universal
nonzero statement.

The solved modes are ground states of separately declared external wells.
There is no common nonlinear field equation, simultaneous source solution,
interaction energy, or stability theorem. Grid doubling checks one output but
the source performs no domain refinement or eigenpair residual. The result is
a toy matrix example, not a proved three-condensate or multisoliton sector.

## GC3.5b: Geometry

One declared equally spaced triple yields a smaller nonzero quartet than one
declared unequal triple. No symmetry theorem, exhaustive geometry class,
domain study, or physical map converts that comparison into a prediction that
generation geometry is unequal. The numeric values remain reproduction
evidence only.

## GC3.6: Corrected Phase Budget

The source uses `N` both for the number of order parameters and the dimension
of the relative unitary matrix. These are independent inputs. If K source
phases share one declared global shift, their coordinate supply is K-1. The
generic matrix quotient demand from C-MIX-002 is `(N-1)(N-2)/2`. Thus at
matrix dimension N=3, K=2 already supplies one relative phase.

P210 gives an exact realization: a complex symmetric Fourier-Takagi matrix is
the sum of two real symmetric matrices at phases zero and pi/2 and has a
nonzero quartet relative to a real diagonal sector. This is matrix algebra,
not a physical source-count sufficiency theorem, because the map from source
coordinates to quotient phases can be rank deficient.

## GC3.7 and GC3.8: Guards

The complex control confirms the numeric pipeline can return a nonzero
quartet; the exact Fourier example removes seed and threshold dependence. The
pinned source imports only NumPy, SymPy, and one SciPy eigensolver and performs
no file or network reads, so it contains no measured flavor comparator. Its
selected globals-name intersection alone would not prove that reachability
claim in a less isolated program.

## Terminal Disposition

GC3 maps its conditional profile, real-mode, overlap, matrix, rephasing, and
new common-phase algebra to
`[C-QBL-001,C-QBL-003,C-OVL-001,C-MIX-001,C-MIX-002,C-MIX-003]` and is
otherwise qualified. Excluded scope includes a universal one-condensate CP
no-go, Yukawa or CKM identity, physical CP operation, required three-source
count, stable coexistence, unequal-spacing prediction, observed phase,
generation map, and substrate realization.
