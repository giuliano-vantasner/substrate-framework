# P241 Literature Audit

Scope: sources the manuscript relies on, checked against what they actually
support. Judged for THIS review only; not a survey.

## [1] 1+1D sine–Gordon synthesis (Fulceri, 14 Aug 2026)

* Cited for: breathers/localization in an elastic continuum; boost closure.
* Verified here: sine–Gordon admits the exact breather used as our Part-B
  control (measured ω = 0.80002 vs exact √(1−η²) = 0.8; energy retained
  100% over >20 periods). The manuscript's reliance on this class is sound —
  but note it is a NONCONVEX potential, which is precisely what the 3+1D
  displayed U(u) lacks (S06 finding).

## [2] 2+1D NLKG treatment (20 Aug 2026)

* Cited for: the rotating minimal model and impedance matching in d=2.
* Verified here: equations (58)-(60) of the present paper are VERBATIM the
  published 2+1D minimal rotating model, including its explicit
  physical-eigenvalue determinant convention with Jacobian factors
  (λ_r=A^{rr}, λ_θ=r²A^{θθ}, λ_φ=r²sin²θ A^{φφ}). The 3+1D Section 9 drops
  that convention and its printed determinant matches neither convention nor
  coordinate product (S21). Provenance rename required (S22 repair).

## Derrick's theorem / no-go literature (Derrick 1964; Coleman Q-balls; standard references)

* Cited for: deferring static-existence questions.
* Finding: the cited no-gos concern charged or time-dependent classes; the
  relevant obstruction for the paper's uncharged static class is plain
  Derrick scaling E(λ)=E_g/λ+E_p/λ³, strictly decreasing in d=3 (S05). The
  deferral sentence should cite the direct argument instead.

## Equivalence principle literature (Einstein 1907; modern formulations)

* The headline compares itself to Einstein 1907. The verified content is a
  *scoped analogue*: universality of free fall across M0 for massive lumps +
  doubled null deflection + time dilation/contraction in an induced optical
  metric. This is a continuum-acoustics realization, not a new fundamental
  principle; the comparison supports deletion/scoping of the headline (S23).

## Numerical-oscillon literature (small-amplitude oscillons, oscillillons)

* Consistent with our Part-A finding: sublinear binding requires softening;
  convex/hardening powers do not support long-lived localized oscillons in
  3+1D. No contradiction with the cited lower-dimensional syntheses.
